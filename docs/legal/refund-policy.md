**Who sells your appointment:** the clinic or doctor you book. We run the booking and process refunds on the clinic's behalf, under this policy.

## Short version (shown before you pay)

**Free cancellation up to 2 hours before your appointment. Full refund if the doctor doesn't join, or if the clinic cancels.**

## When you get your money back

| What happened | Online consultation | Clinic visit (paid in the app) |
|---|---|---|
| You cancel **2 hours or more** before | Full refund | Full refund |
| You cancel less than 2 hours before, or don't attend within 10 minutes of the start time | No refund | No refund |
| You change the time 2 hours or more before | Free, once | Free, once |
| **The doctor doesn't join within 10 minutes** | **Full refund, automatic** | — |
| The clinic or doctor cancels, or the clinic closes | Full refund, automatic, or a free new time (your choice) | Same |
| The call fails for technical reasons and the doctor records it | Full refund or a free new time (your choice) | — |
| The doctor says you need to be seen in person | The fee counts towards a clinic visit with the same doctor within 7 days, or you get a 50% refund | — |
| Money was taken but no booking was made | Returned automatically | Same |
| You were charged twice | The extra payment is returned automatically | Same |

Some clinics offer more generous terms. If so, the app shows them before you pay. No clinic can offer worse terms.

## How refunds work

- Refunds start within 1 hour and go back to the way you paid (card, UPI or net banking).
- Banks usually take 5–7 working days. The app shows the status and a reference number.
- If a refund is late or does not arrive, contact our Grievance Officer (see the [Grievance Redressal](grievance-redressal.md) page). We acknowledge within 24 hours and aim to resolve within 7 days.

## Fairness

If the clinic or doctor cancels, you always get a full refund or a free new time.

> **Drafting note (remove before publishing).** This draft does **not yet** meet the E-Commerce Rules 2020 test as written.
>
> **The rule** (rule 4(6)): an e-commerce entity may impose a cancellation charge on a consumer only if it "also bears similar charges" when it cancels unilaterally. A refund only returns the patient's own money; it is not a charge borne. So keeping 100% of the fee on a late cancellation or no-show (row 2), while a late clinic cancellation or doctor no-show costs the clinic nothing beyond the refund, is probably **not symmetric**.
>
> **Options [CONFIRM founder; VALIDATE lawyer]:**
>
> - **A. Matching compensation (recommended):** if the clinic cancels less than 2 hours before, or the doctor doesn't join, the patient gets the full refund **plus** compensation equal to the late-cancellation charge. In mode B the clinic funds it, under the Provider Agreement.
> - **B. Lower patient charge:** late cancellation or no-show keeps a smaller share (for example 50%). Late clinic cancellations add matching compensation.
> - **C. No cancellation charge:** always refund in full. Simplest to comply with, but gives no protection against no-shows.
>
> **For the lawyer:**
>
> - whether rule 4(6) applies to the platform, the clinic (seller of record in mode B), or both;
> - whether 100% forfeiture is reasonable under consumer law;
> - whether the 50% outcome in row "The doctor says you need to be seen in person" is fair, since the doctor makes that call.
>
> Whatever is chosen changes RF rules in [refund-policy.v1.draft.yaml](../payments/refund-policy.v1.draft.yaml) and clause B4 of the [Provider Agreement](provider-agreement.md).
