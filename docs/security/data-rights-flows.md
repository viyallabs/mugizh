# Data-rights flows: access and export, correction, erasure, nominee (S-060)

**Phase P06 · Epic EP08 · Depends on:** S-057 · **Contract refs:** K-DP-07 (also K-DP-02, K-DP-05, K-CS-05) · **Edge cases:** E-DP-03, E-DP-10, E-DP-15 (also E-SEC-10) · **Compliance:** C02 (also C30) · **Date:** 2026-11-15
**Status:** In Testing. Flows are designed. **The acceptance criterion also needs the lawyer to check them (S-009)**; each [VALIDATE] item is listed in section 7.

**Who this is for:** engineering (S-142 queue, S-143 patient flows), the Grievance Officer, and the lawyer. The policy, timelines and wording are in [data-rights-and-grievance.md](../privacy/data-rights-and-grievance.md). **This document is how the system does it.**

## 1. Legal map (DPDP Act 2023; DPDP Rules 2025, duties from 13 May 2027)

| Right | DPDP | What the law asks | What we add |
|---|---|---|---|
| Access | s.11 | A **summary** of the personal data processed and the processing; who it was shared with | A full download of the medical record (our feature, not a portability right) |
| Correction, completion, updating | s.12(1), (2) | Correct inaccurate data; complete and update it | Clinical corrections through the treating doctor, as new versions (K-CS-05) |
| Erasure | s.12(3), s.8(7) | Erase unless keeping it is needed for the purpose, **or required by law** | Legal-retention records are **restricted, not deleted** (E-DP-03) |
| Grievance | s.13; rule 14 | A way to complain; a response within the published period (the Rules allow up to 90 days) | Our targets: 24 h acknowledgement, 15 days for rights requests, 7 days for complaints |
| Nominate | s.14 | A nominee may act if the person dies or becomes incapable | Four-eyes verification (E-DP-10) |

**Before 13 May 2027**, the SPDI Rules 2011 apply to health data (N1). They are covered by the same flows.

## 2. Common steps for every request

1. **Request:** in the app (SC-18, SC-52), or to the Grievance Officer by email or post.
2. **Identity:** signed-in session plus **step-up OTP** (E-SEC-10). If the phone was lost: the account-recovery check (S-115). **Never Aadhaar.**
3. **Record:** a `RightsRequest` with type, reference, received time, due date and status. Due dates:
   - **15 days** for rights requests;
   - **72 hours for a copy of medical records** (IMC Regulations 2002, reg. 1.3.2), including restricted records after account closure. In the app the patient can download their own records immediately. A 24-hour acknowledgement with the due date (SMS-12).
4. **Do it** (sections 3 to 6), with a `RightsTask` per module where needed.
5. **Close:** the outcome is told to the person (SMS-13 plus in-app detail). Evidence kept under RET-09. The S-142 queue shows timers; it alerts at 10 days and when breached.

## 3. Access and export (E-DP-15)

| Part | Content | Format |
|---|---|---|
| Summary (s.11) | Data categories held, purposes, consents with dates and versions, who data was shared with (processors list, doctors with grants) | In-app page + PDF |
| Record | Profile, appointments, consult records, notes and prescriptions (every version), uploads, reminders history, payments and refunds, the access log | ZIP: PDFs + JSON (FHIR R4 bundle from v2.0, S-083) |
| Not included | Credentials (PER-K); internal security signals; other people's data (for example a doctor's private notes about their own practice) | — |

**Delivery:**
- The archive is generated by a worker, **encrypted at rest** with a file key, and offered **only inside the signed-in app**.
- The download link is user-bound, **single use** and **expires in 24 hours** (S-171).
- If the user wants to send the archive elsewhere, the app can produce a **password-protected ZIP** (AES-256), with the password shown separately in the app [CONFIRM].

## 4. Correction

| Data | How |
|---|---|
| Profile and contact details | The user edits them directly; a phone change needs step-up and re-verification |
| Clinical content (notes, diagnosis, prescription) | The request goes to the **treating doctor's** queue. The doctor adds a corrected **new version** (the original stays, K-CS-05), or declines with a reason. If declined, the patient's **statement of disagreement** is attached to the record and shown with it [VALIDATE]. |
| Doctor no longer on the platform | The Grievance Officer contacts the clinic. If the record cannot be corrected, a statement of disagreement is attached. |
| Verification data (doctors) | Re-verification with new evidence |

## 5. Erasure (account closure) (E-DP-03, K-DP-07)

```mermaid
sequenceDiagram
  autonumber
  participant P as Patient
  participant R as records (rights orchestrator)
  participant M as Every module + scheduler-core
  participant V as Vendor adapters
  participant K as Key store
  P->>R: Close account (step-up OTP)
  R-->>P: What will be deleted, what is kept and until when; undo window
  Note over R: Undo window (proposed 7 days) [VALIDATE]; patient can cancel
  R->>M: rights.erasure_requested (request ref, subject ref)
  M->>M: DEL-ERASE non-retained data; DEL-RESTRICT clinical records (RET-02)
  M-->>R: rights.task_done per module
  R->>V: requestDeletion(subject) per vendor (DEL-VENDOR)
  V-->>R: Confirmations
  Note over R,K: Legal-minimum hold (RET-18): data restricted, no staff access except a verified legal order
  R->>K: When the hold ends: destroy patient key (care keys kept until each RET-02 period ends)
  R-->>P: Erasure report (SMS-13 + email/in-app copy until closure completes)
```

**What happens to each kind of data:**

| Data | Action |
|---|---|
| Profile, contact, uploads, reminders, preferences | **At once:** hidden, processing stopped, removed from search and caches. **Locked under the legal-minimum hold (RET-18):** registration data 180 days after closure (Intermediary Rules 3(1)(h)); from 13 May 2027, personal data and processing logs at least 1 year from the last processing (DPDP Rules 8(3)). **Then deleted**; the patient key is destroyed, so copies in backups become unreadable (encryption design §4) |
| Analytics link | Deleted at once (analytics data is pseudonymous and not covered by the hold) [VALIDATE] |
| Notes, prescriptions, PDFs, consult records (RET-02) | **Restricted:** hidden from the app; kept encrypted under the care key; reachable only by the treating doctor (T2), the patient on request through the Grievance Officer, or a legal order; **care key destroyed when the period ends**, unless a legal hold applies |
| Payments and invoices (RET-04) | Restricted for 8 years [CA]; then deleted |
| Consent records (RET-05), access log (RET-06), rights request (RET-09) | Restricted for their periods (they prove we acted lawfully) |
| Audit events | Kept; pseudonymous once the mapping is gone (audit design §5) |

- **Step 1 is done** (within the 15-day target) when every module has hidden or restricted its data and reported.
- **Step 2 is done** when the hold ends: every module has deleted its data, every vendor has confirmed deletion (or it is escalated after 30 days), and the patient key is destroyed.
- **Staff cannot open held data.** Only a verified legal order under the [lawful-request procedure](../legal/lawful-requests-procedure.md) releases it, with an audit event.
- **The report** tells the person what was deleted, what was kept, why and until when. This is the E-DP-03 explanation.

**Other ways erasure starts:**
- **Inactivity:** an account with no sign-in for 3 years [CONFIRM]. We send a **48-hour advance notice** to the phone and email first, then run the same flow. The DPDP Rules set a 48-hour notice for their listed classes; we apply it to all inactive accounts.
- **Under-18 found** (CH-12).
- **v0 pilot data** at RET-14.

## 6. Nominee (E-DP-10)

1. **Name a nominee:** the user adds name, relation and phone (RET-15). The nominee gets an SMS saying they were named (no health details) [CONFIRM].
2. **When it is used:** the nominee contacts the Grievance Officer. They give proof (death certificate, or a medical or court document showing incapacity) and an OTP to the nominee's own phone.
3. **Check:** **two staff members** verify it (four-eyes) and record the decision with the evidence.
4. **What the nominee can do:** access, correction, erasure, or keeping the record, as DPDP allows. It is a JIT-style grant, time-limited and audited.
5. **Without a nominee:** legal heirs follow the legal route (court order or succession document), handled by the Grievance Officer [VALIDATE].

## 7. For the lawyer [VALIDATE]

1. The format of the s.11 summary under the Rules, and whether our download meets it.
2. Statement of disagreement for clinical corrections the doctor declines.
3. A 7-day undo window before hard erasure (threat model TM-33). Is it acceptable under DPDP?
4. 48-hour notice before erasing inactive accounts (we apply it to all of them).
5. Nominee proof standards; heirs without a nominee.
6. **Fiduciary question:** whether the clinic and doctor, rather than us, must answer correction and erasure requests for their clinical records (data inventory open item 2).
